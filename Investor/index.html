01 · The opportunity

Waste treatment is becoming more expensive. Construction needs better materials.

Municipalities, waste operators and industrial companies face rising transport, disposal and landfill costs. At the same time, construction and infrastructure markets are under growing pressure to reduce virgin raw material use, increase recycled content and adopt lower-carbon solutions.

♻️

A costly waste problem

Mixed and difficult waste streams are expensive to transport, treat and landfill, creating pressure on municipalities, recyclers and industrial operators.

🏗️

Value for material buyers

Competitive pricing and aggregates tailored to factory requirements. Lightweight aggregates for blocks and lightweight concrete help reduce building weight. Separate aggregate products serve under-floor applications, with thermal and acoustic insulation benefits in suitable applications.

02 · Business model

Two revenue streams. Project-specific partnerships.

eco BRICK earns from waste treatment and product sales. Facility funding and cost-sharing arrangements differ by project.

Waste-treatment fees → production → product sales

Company operations

  • Waste-treatment fees: eco BRICK receives payment for accepting suitable waste, at a lower cost to the contractor than the landfill alternative.
  • Product sales: eco BRICK sells the resulting products to customers who have evaluated them and found them suitable for their needs.

Project-specific partnerships

eco BRICK provides an IP-use license, leads setup, manufactures production machines and supplies the appropriate binder at a pre-agreed price. Its contribution to facility costs varies by project; all project-management costs on eco BRICK’s side are funded by the company.

  • Every facility must purchase its binder from eco BRICK, which exclusively manufactures it. This keeps binder production under company control and provides an ongoing revenue stream from supply to facilities.
  • Participation in the facility’s profits
  • Product sales managed under the eco BRICK umbrella

04 · Regional scale strategy

Distributed production instead of one centralized mega-facility.

The strategy is to place regional production near waste sources and end markets, reducing the economic burden of transporting large volumes of low-value waste over long distances.

Demonstrate operations → establish site economics → advance partner investment in expansion.

07 · Capital plan

$2M to initiate the program. $10M to fund the overall plan.

The first $2M stage advances three facilities and funds the engineering, management and operational capabilities needed to execute the program. eco BRICK contributes to facility costs under a different arrangement at each site and funds all of its own project-management costs. The $2M advances this work; it does not represent full funding of all three facilities. The overall fundraising target remains $10M.

First-stage priorities

  • Haifa Municipal Association: add a production machine to increase capacity and support demonstration of site profitability.
  • Hiriya / Dan Municipal Association: advance planning, equipment production and procurement, and staffing.
  • Ashdod / HAPAA agricultural farm: establish an initial one-machine facility with ancillary equipment funded by eco BRICK.
  • Execution team: phase in employment for engineering, management and operations within the first-stage budget, with broader team expansion after the full raise.

Operating evidence within the six-month program

  • Waste intake and treatment-fee revenue
  • Availability and throughput
  • Energy and labor requirements
  • Cost per tonne
  • Product sales and repeat purchasing
  • Gross margin and site-level economics
$2M

First stage — Initiate the program

The six-month program combines equipment procurement, installation and commissioning with ongoing production, team growth and progress at the other two sites. Operating evidence is intended to support the next fundraising stage and planned partner investment in expansion.

$8M

Remaining funding — Complete the capital plan

The remaining $8M is part of the overall $10M fundraising target required to fund the full program.

Expansion is intended to add machines and production, packaging and marketing equipment after capability is demonstrated. Partner investment is planned, not guaranteed. Facility costs weigh heavily on a one-machine operation; under the model assumptions, profitability improves materially at three machines. Five machines are the reference scenario, not a capacity limit: sites can expand to 10, 15 or more machines, subject to site conditions, feedstock, demand and the costs of each configuration.

$10M total target = $2M first stage + $8M remaining funding
