01 · The opportunity

Waste treatment is becoming more expensive. Construction needs better materials.

Municipalities, waste operators and industrial companies face rising transport, disposal and landfill costs. At the same time, construction and infrastructure markets are under growing pressure to reduce virgin raw material use, increase recycled content and adopt lower-carbon solutions.

♻️

A costly waste problem

Mixed and difficult waste streams are expensive to transport, treat and landfill, creating pressure on municipalities, recyclers and industrial operators.

🏗️

A materials opportunity

eco BRICK converts suitable waste streams into engineered raw materials and finished products with commercial value for construction and infrastructure.

02 · Business model

A dual-revenue model.

eco BRICK is designed to generate revenue at both ends of the process — first from waste treatment, then from product sales.

Get paid to receive waste → process it → sell the resulting product
💰

Waste treatment revenue

Municipalities, waste operators, recycling facilities and industrial companies can pay a treatment fee for accepting and processing suitable waste streams.

🏭

Product revenue

Processed waste is converted into engineered products including cold asphalt, lightweight aggregates, concrete applications and specialized infrastructure products.

04 · Regional scale strategy

Distributed production instead of one centralized mega-facility.

The strategy is to place regional production near waste sources and end markets, reducing the economic burden of transporting large volumes of low-value waste over long distances.

One site proves the economics. · Five machines maximize each site. · Multiple regional sites create scale.

06 · Capital plan

The $2M round activates the first phase of a broader $10M scale-up plan.

The current Seed round is intended to launch commercial-scale production, capture six months of real operating data, advance international pilots and strengthen IP — not to finance the final form of the company.

What the $2M activates

  • Two new full-scale production machines
  • Continuous mixing and screening systems
  • Licensed commercial production facility
  • Dedicated operating and production team
  • Hiriya site development
  • Pilots in Singapore, India, France, Sweden and the United States
  • Patent filings and IP protection

What six months must prove

  • Waste intake and treatment-fee revenue
  • Availability and throughput
  • Energy and labor requirements
  • Cost per ton
  • Product sales and repeat purchasing
  • Gross margin and site-level economics
