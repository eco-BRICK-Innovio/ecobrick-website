01 · The opportunity

Waste treatment is becoming more expensive. Construction needs better materials.

Municipalities, waste operators and industrial companies face rising transport, disposal and landfill costs. At the same time, construction and infrastructure markets are under growing pressure to reduce virgin raw material use, increase recycled content and adopt lower-carbon solutions.

♻️

A costly waste problem

Mixed and difficult waste streams are expensive to transport, treat and landfill, creating pressure on municipalities, recyclers and industrial operators.

🏗️

A materials opportunity

eco BRICK converts suitable waste streams into engineered raw materials and finished products with commercial value for construction and infrastructure.

02 · Business model

Two revenue streams. Partner-funded expansion.

eco BRICK earns from waste treatment and product sales, and expands through joint ventures funded by its partners.

Waste-treatment fees → production → product sales

Company operations

  • Waste-treatment fees: eco BRICK receives payment for accepting suitable waste, at a lower cost to the contractor than the landfill alternative.
  • Product sales: eco BRICK sells the resulting products to customers who have evaluated them and found them suitable for their needs.

Partner-funded joint ventures

The partner finances the facility. eco BRICK provides an IP-use license, leads setup, manufactures production machines and supplies the appropriate binder at a pre-agreed price.

  • Ongoing binder sales to the facility
  • Participation in the facility’s profits
  • Product sales managed under the eco BRICK umbrella

04 · Regional scale strategy

Distributed production instead of one centralized mega-facility.

The strategy is to place regional production near waste sources and end markets, reducing the economic burden of transporting large volumes of low-value waste over long distances.

One site proves the economics. · Five machines maximize each site. · Multiple regional sites create scale.

Haifa Municipal Association

First regional pilot production facility

Current stage: NIS 3.5 million support approved; agreement not yet signed and funds not yet transferred; feedstock and site preparation

Project details

The Haifa Municipal Association, comprising 18 local authorities including the City of Haifa, has approved support for a regional eco BRICK pilot facility. The agreement has not yet been signed and funds have not yet been transferred.

Together with the Association, we are working to identify a building and land for the facility and advance agreements with local waste suppliers and manufacturing plants. The Association’s CEO has indicated that support is expected to increase to NIS 4M.

  • No equity dilution
  • No share of eco BRICK product revenues
  • Repayment linked to production volume until the support amount is returned
