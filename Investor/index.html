01 · The opportunity

Waste treatment is becoming more expensive. Construction needs better materials.

Municipalities, waste operators and industrial companies face rising transport, disposal and landfill costs. At the same time, construction and infrastructure markets are under growing pressure to reduce virgin raw material use, increase recycled content and adopt lower-carbon solutions.

♻️

A costly waste problem

Mixed and difficult waste streams are expensive to transport, treat and landfill, creating pressure on municipalities, recyclers and industrial operators.

🏗️

Value for material buyers

Competitive pricing and aggregates tailored to factory requirements. Lightweight aggregates for blocks and lightweight concrete help reduce building weight. Other aggregate products serve underfloor fill and insulation applications, with thermal and acoustic benefits where suitable.

Our markets span waste treatment, construction aggregates and asphalt products, starting in Israel and extending internationally. Market data and sources are available in the Data Room to approved investors.

02 · Business model

Two economic layers: facility revenue and eco BRICK revenue.

Each production facility earns operating revenue. eco BRICK earns separately as the technology partner, exclusive binder supplier, commercial platform and—where a joint venture applies—equity partner.

Waste intake + product sales → facility revenue → operating profit → shareholder distribution
Facility-level revenue

How the production facility earns

  • Waste-treatment fees: the waste supplier, contractor or operator pays the facility to accept and treat suitable waste.
  • Product sales: the facility sells the resulting aggregates, construction materials and asphalt products to customers.

These revenues belong to the operating facility before operating costs, financing and profit distribution.

eco BRICK revenue

How eco BRICK earns from the network

  1. Binder sales: each facility purchases the appropriate proprietary binder from eco BRICK at an agreed price.
  2. Sales commission: eco BRICK receives a commission on products sold under the eco BRICK commercial umbrella.
  3. Equity profit share: where a 50/50 JV structure applies, eco BRICK owns 50% of the production facility and receives 50% of its distributable profits.

Binder sales and sales commissions are company revenue. The 50% profit share applies only where it is agreed as part of a 50/50 JV; the exact economics vary by project.

Different projects, different partnership structures

The commercial logic is consistent, but ownership, funding and repayment terms are negotiated separately for each facility.

Municipal support model

Haifa Municipal Association

  • NIS 3.5 million in support has been approved; the agreement is not yet signed and funds have not yet been transferred.
  • The support does not create equity dilution.
  • The Association does not receive a share of eco BRICK product revenue.

Proposed 50/50 JV

Ashdod · HAPAA

  • The production facility is proposed to be owned 50% by eco BRICK and 50% by HAPAA.

  • Following demonstrated capability, HAPAA is expected to invest in additional machines and site equipment.
  • Under the proposed JV, eco BRICK earns from binder supply, sales commissions and 50% of distributable facility profits.

The Ashdod structure is proposed and remains subject to the formal procurement process and final agreements.

03 · Technology & products

Different waste streams. Products tailored to real applications.

Proprietary binders. Adaptable formulations. Modular production.

eco BRICK combines proprietary binders, a growing formulation knowledge base and modular production technology to convert different waste streams into materials tailored to construction and infrastructure applications.

Materials expertise and software tools help match waste inputs to product requirements, supporting a range of products from a shared technology platform.

Additional applications

Cement-free blocks: waste-based construction system designed for assembly without conventional cement, adhesive or steel reinforcement. Approximately 99% waste-derived material.

Pumpable tunnel concrete: long-distance pumpable concrete for tunnel-sealing applications, using aggregates produced from approximately 99% waste-derived material.

Heavy-duty cold asphalt: rapid road repair mix designed for routes carrying very heavy vehicles, including tanks. Approximately 95% waste-derived material.

06 · Capital plan

A milestone-led path from commissioning to regional scale.

The $10M plan is structured in two stages: an initial execution round across Haifa, Hiriya and Ashdod, followed by capital tied to commissioning, operating evidence and site-level economics.

First-stage priorities

  • Haifa Municipal Association: add a production machine to increase capacity and support demonstration of site profitability.
  • Hiriya / Dan Municipal Association: advance planning, equipment production and procurement, and staffing.
  • Ashdod / HAPAA site: establish an initial one-machine facility with ancillary equipment funded by eco BRICK.
  • Execution team: phase in employment for engineering, management and operations within the first-stage budget, with broader team expansion after the full raise.

Evidence to establish during the first six months

Through setup, commissioning and ongoing operation, we aim to establish:

  • Waste intake and treatment-fee revenue
  • Availability and throughput
  • Energy and labor requirements
  • Cost per tonne
  • Product sales and repeat purchasing
  • Gross margin and site-level economics

Operating data will be gathered as equipment is commissioned and production develops during the six-month program.

$2M

First stage — Initiate the program

The six-month program combines equipment procurement, installation and commissioning with ongoing production, team growth and parallel progress at the remaining sites. Operating evidence is intended to support the next fundraising stage and planned partner investment in expansion.

$8M

Follow-on funding — Scale validated sites

Capital for additional machines, site expansion, packaging equipment and commercialization, subject to operating milestones, site conditions and partner participation.

Regional expansion

The strategy is to place regional production near waste sources and end markets, reducing the economic burden of transporting large volumes of low-value waste over long distances.

How sites expand

Expansion is intended to add production machines and packaging equipment, alongside commercialization activities, after capability is demonstrated. Partner investment is planned, not guaranteed. Facility costs weigh heavily on a one-machine operation; under the model assumptions, profitability improves materially at three machines. Five machines are the reference scenario, not a capacity limit: sites can expand to 10, 15 or more machines, subject to site conditions, feedstock, demand and the costs of each configuration.
