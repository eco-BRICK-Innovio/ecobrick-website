Business model, project context & fundraising

eco BRICK is designed to generate revenue at both ends of the process: fees for receiving and treating suitable waste streams, and sales of the engineered materials and products made from them for construction and infrastructure applications. Locating production near waste sources and end markets is intended to connect waste-treatment needs with demand for practical material applications.

In joint projects, eco BRICK provides an IP-use license, leads setup, manufactures machines and supplies binder. Its revenues include machine and binder sales and participation in facility profits, according to each project’s arrangement; product sales are managed under the eco BRICK umbrella.

The route to market is built around activities with organizations addressing defined needs at meaningful scale: the IDF and Israel Ministry of Defense, the Haifa Municipal Association, Ashdod, Jerusalem, the Western Negev Cluster, Hiriya and Hevra Kadisha Rishon LeZion, alongside the development of partnerships and pilots in Israel and abroad, including India and Singapore.

This room provides supporting evidence for reviewing those activities: pilots and applications, funding documents, notices of intent to engage, recommendations and planning materials. Each activity is at its own stage of development; a pilot, funding approval or procurement notice does not by itself establish a completed sale. The Interactive Business Plan explores production-site economics, while the materials here support review of the practical and commercial path to deployment.

The first $2 million stage, within a $10 million fundraising target, advances facilities with the Haifa Municipal Association, Hiriya / Dan Municipal Association and HAPAA in Ashdod, alongside company-team growth. eco BRICK contributes to facility costs differently at each site and funds all its own project-management costs. Demonstrated operations and profitability are intended to support planned partner investment in expansion; that investment is not guaranteed. Five machines are a model reference scenario, not a capacity limit; expansion to 10, 15 or more machines is possible, with economics assessed for each configuration.
